you will not see other branches' employees, attendance or payroll.

## 2. What you can see

Four tabs:

| Tab | What you use it for |
|---|---|
| **Clock In/Out** | The daily time clock |
| **Attendance** | Checking and correcting the day's records |
| **Payroll** | Your branch's totals for the period |
| **Employees** | Your branch's staff list |

Pay rates, 13th month, system settings and the audit log are the owner's and
do not appear for you.

---

## 3. The time clock — the important one

The Clock In/Out screen is the day's most important screen. Everything in
payroll is built from what it records, so a missed punch becomes a pay
problem later.

There are **four buttons**, in two pairs:

**Shift**
- **Clock In** — arriving
- **Clock Out** — leaving at the end of the shift

**Overtime**
- **OT In** — starting overtime, such as waiting to unload a delivery
- **OT Out** — finishing overtime

They must be used in order: Clock In, then Clock Out, then OT In, then
OT Out. The overtime pair is separate on purpose — the wait between clocking
out and starting to unload is not paid, and every minute of the overtime pair
counts as overtime whatever the clock says.

Each employee taps their own **PIN**. PINs are personal: one person clocking
in for another puts wrong hours into someone's pay.

---

## 4. Daily routine

**Through the day:** make sure everyone clocks in and out on their own PIN.

**End of day:** open Attendance and check the day.

- Anyone with a clock-in and **no clock-out** — fix it before you leave. That
  day earns nothing until it is corrected.
- Anyone who did not clock in at all — record the reason (absent, leave,
  rest day, half day).
- Late arrivals and early departures are calculated automatically. You do not
  need to compute anything.

Every correction asks for a **reason**. Write a real one — it is kept on
record and is what answers the question later if a figure is queried.

---

## 5. Attendance you should not fix yourself

Tell the owner instead of correcting these:

- A day that needs a **rate** change
- Anything you are not certain about — a guess entered as fact is harder to
  unpick than a question asked at the time
- Disputes over hours between an employee and a supervisor

---

## 6. Payroll tab

You can see your branch's totals for the period — daily, weekly,
semi-monthly, and the payslip view.

This is for checking, not for computing. Compare it against your own record
of who worked, and raise anything that looks wrong **before** the cut-off:
1–15, and 16 to the end of the month.

You cannot add allowances, deductions or cash advances. Send those to the
owner with the employee's name, the amount, the date and what it is for.

---

## 7. Employees tab

Your branch's staff list, with their shift times.

Tell the owner when someone **joins**, **leaves**, or **changes shift**. New
staff need a PIN before they can clock in at all.

---

## 8. Before each cut-off

A short checklist for the 15th and the end of the month:

1. Every day in the period has complete clock-ins and clock-outs.
2. Absences, leaves and rest days are marked with a reason.
3. Overtime pairs are recorded for anyone who worked past their shift.
4. The Payroll tab matches your own record of who worked.
5. Allowances, deductions and cash advances have been sent to the owner.

---

## 9. Questions

Anything about pay rates, deductions, 13th month or system settings goes to
the owner. Your job here is to make sure the **attendance is right** —
everything else is computed from it.
